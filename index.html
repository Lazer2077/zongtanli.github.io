Ph.D. Student · College of Engineering, University of Georgia

I work on robot learning and embodied AI, with one question running through it: how can a learned controller keep improving after deployment without breaking what already works?

My research covers sequential skill acquisition for humanoid controllers, imitation learning that runs faster than the demonstrations, reward-free online adaptation to changing dynamics, and recurrent policies for partially observed control. It draws on optimal control (NMPC), simulation, and hands-on robotic systems.

Research

Continual improvement of learned humanoid controllers without regression

ICRA 2027 · under review

A sequential update mechanism that adds recovery skills to an already-competent controller at measured failures. Instead of only freezing parameters, it constrains where each new skill may act, so previously successful trajectories are reproduced exactly. I derived a finite-trajectory condition under which each update preserves the previous controller.

5 → 33/48recovery on withheld perturbations (CMU + NPMP)
20 → 33/48recovery on Unitree G1 with SONIC
0base successes lost after two updates

Imitation-learned robot policies faster than the human demonstrations

ICRA 2027 · under review

When re-timed demonstrations are sped up 2–3×, success collapses because merged commands exceed the displacement the low-level controller can realize per period. A one-parameter, linear-time re-timing rule merges commands only while they remain realizable. It keeps the demonstrated geometry, speeds up where the demonstrator left slack, and lands within 0.1% of the exact shortest-path optimum.

96% vs 60%success on Robomimic Can, vs. uniform 2× speed-up
71% @ 2.3×of 429 clips on a G1 whole-body tracker (uniform: 51% @ 2×)

Reward-free online adaptation of deployed policies

ICLR 2027 · under review

Efference-copy adaptation keeps the task policy frozen and corrects its outgoing commands online when robot dynamics change after training. A lightweight two-timescale linear adapter is driven by the mismatch between a frozen forward model's prediction and the observed response. It needs no reward, fault labels, or policy gradients. It outperformed perturbation-supervised RMA on nine reaching perturbations and adapted at least 40× faster than a return-driven residual.

Online Adaptation · Adaptive Control · Forward Models · Frozen Policies · MuJoCo

Sample-efficient memory for long-horizon partially observed control

AAAI 2027 · under review

A group-structured, quantum-inspired recurrent policy with observation-conditioned phase rotations and a Born-rule readout. I proved that a two-channel recurrence exactly represents cyclic-group state regardless of episode horizon. It generalizes from training horizons of ≤14 steps to 1,600-step evaluation with only 337–433 parameters, outperforming GRU, LSTM and Transformer policies.

Reinforcement Learning · POMDPs · Recurrent Policies · Complex-Valued Networks

Energy-optimal control for connected and automated electric vehicles

ACC 2025

A CasADi-based nonlinear MPC framework coupling vehicle dynamics and powertrain energy management. It cut energy consumption by 9.1% relative to a trajectory-based baseline while keeping tracking objectives.

NMPC · CasADi · Connected and Automated Vehicles
