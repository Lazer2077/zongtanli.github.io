About Me

I'm a Ph.D. student in the College of Engineering at the University of Georgia, currently visiting the Georgia Institute of Technology. I work on robot learning and embodied AI.

My work is about learned robot controllers that stay dependable after deployment. I want them to get faster, recover from new failures, and adapt when the robot's dynamics change, without breaking anything they could already do. I build on reinforcement and imitation learning and use optimal control, simulation and real-robot experiments.

What I'm working on now

  • Faster-than-demonstration imitation. Robots taught by teleoperation inherit the operator's slow pace. I re-time demonstrations only within what the low-level controller can physically execute, so policies run faster without the usual collapse in success.
  • Continual skill acquisition for humanoids. Adding recovery skills to a working whole-body controller without losing any behavior it already had.
  • Reward-free online adaptation. Correcting a frozen policy's commands online when the robot's dynamics change after training.
  • Memory under partial observability. Compact recurrent policies that keep track of hidden state over long horizons.

Current focus

Faster imitation by re-timing demonstrations within the controller limit

ICRA 2027 · under review

A policy trained on human teleoperation inherits the operator's pace. Speeding up the demonstrations works only until success collapses at 2–3×. I show the collapse has a mechanical cause: merged commands ask for more displacement per control period than the low-level controller can deliver, and the share of such pairs in the training data predicts where the retrained policy fails. The fix is a one-parameter rule that merges consecutive commands only while their sum stays realizable. It keeps the demonstrated path and speeds up only where the demonstrator left slack.

Continual improvement of learned humanoid controllers without regression

ICRA 2027 · under review

A sequential update mechanism that adds recovery skills to an already-competent controller at measured failures. Instead of only freezing parameters, it constrains where each new skill may act, so previously successful trajectories are reproduced exactly. I derived a finite-trajectory condition under which each update preserves the previous controller.

Reward-free online adaptation of deployed policies

ICLR 2027 · under review

Efference-copy adaptation keeps the task policy frozen and corrects its outgoing commands online when robot dynamics change after training. A lightweight two-timescale linear adapter is driven by the mismatch between a frozen forward model's prediction and the observed response. It needs no reward, fault labels, or policy gradients. It outperformed perturbation-supervised RMA on nine reaching perturbations and adapted at least 40× faster than a return-driven residual.

Sample-efficient memory for long-horizon partially observed control

Under review

A group-structured, quantum-inspired recurrent policy with observation-conditioned phase rotations and a Born-rule readout. I proved that a two-channel recurrence exactly represents cyclic-group state regardless of episode horizon. It generalizes from training horizons of ≤14 steps to 1,600-step evaluation with only 337–433 parameters, outperforming GRU, LSTM and Transformer policies.

Reinforcement Learning · POMDPs · Recurrent Policies · Complex-Valued Networks

Energy-optimal control for connected and automated electric vehicles

ACC 2025

A CasADi-based nonlinear MPC framework coupling vehicle dynamics and powertrain energy management. It cut energy consumption by 9.1% relative to a trajectory-based baseline while keeping tracking objectives.
